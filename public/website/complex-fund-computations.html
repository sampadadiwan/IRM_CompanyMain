Fund accounting

Complex fund computations: deterministic at the core. Agentic at the edge.

How CapHive is helping private market fund managers eliminate spreadsheet dependencies, institutionalise complex financial logic, and build the foundation for AI-powered fund operations.

Nov 3, 2026 · 7 min read · Fund accounting

The last mile of fund automation remains surprisingly manual

Private markets have grown significantly in scale, complexity and sophistication. Yet, behind the scenes, some of the most critical financial computations continue to depend on spreadsheets, manual adjustments and the institutional knowledge of a handful of experienced fund accountants.

The challenge is not simply calculating management fees, carried interest, NAVs or investor allocations. It is managing the extraordinary variety of rules, exceptions and interdependencies that arise across funds, investors, share classes, currencies and investment vehicles.

Consider a fund with multiple feeder vehicles, investor-specific side letters, different fee arrangements, complex distribution waterfalls and investments denominated in multiple currencies. A seemingly straightforward fee or carry calculation can involve dozens of interdependent computations, each governed by specific contractual terms.

Traditional fund accounting systems often struggle with this complexity. The result is a familiar operating model: standard calculations happen in the system, while exceptions and more complex logic are handled through Excel, manual overrides and offline reconciliations.

The most complex and consequential computations are often the least automated, least transparent and most dependent on individuals.

At CapHive, we believe that genuine fund automation requires a different architectural approach.

At the heart of the platform is a powerful, configurable computation framework that supports fund-specific algorithms, investor-level rules, multi-vehicle dependencies and sophisticated allocation methodologies.

Importantly, every financial computation is executed deterministically within CapHive's application, not generated probabilistically by an AI model.

Given the same inputs, rules and configuration, the application produces the same results. Computations are supported by traceable calculation logic, explainable allocations and audit trails.

Financial correctness cannot be a matter of probability.

Fund managers need confidence not only in the final number, but also in their ability to explain exactly how that number was derived, without having to depend on individuals.

Every computed number carries the rule that produced it, and the values that went in.

Case study: automating multi-vehicle, multi-currency computations for a multi-billion-dollar PE fund

A particularly compelling example comes from CapHive's work with a multi-billion-dollar private equity fund operating a complex master-feeder structure.

The fund had two feeder vehicles, including one denominated in a different currency, investing through a master fund, and a related SPV.

The challenge

The structure created intricate computational dependencies. Management fees and carried interest calculations at the master fund level needed to reference computations originating in the feeder vehicles, while also accounting for exchange-rate movements and related adjustments.

These were not independent calculations that could be processed in isolation. A change in one vehicle could affect calculations in another, requiring careful coordination across the entire structure.

The fund administrator's existing systems could not natively handle all these interdependencies. Instead, the team relied on spreadsheets and multiple manual overlays to reconcile results and arrive at the required figures. This introduced several operational risks:

  • Manual intervention created opportunities for errors and inconsistencies and weakened auditability.
  • Reconciliations became more time-consuming and difficult to reproduce.
  • Knowledge of unusual scenarios and edge cases was concentrated among a small number of experienced individuals.
  • Changes to calculations required careful coordination across multiple spreadsheets and teams.

The issue was not a lack of accounting expertise. It was an operating architecture that depended excessively on that expertise to compensate for technological limitations.

The CapHive approach

CapHive configured the underlying fee, carry, allocation and currency-adjustment logic directly within its deterministic computation framework.

Computations could reference results across the feeder and master vehicles, apply the appropriate currency treatments and execute the required interdependent calculations without manual spreadsheet overlays. The outcome was a fundamentally more resilient operating model:

  • No manual calculation overlays: complex cross-vehicle calculations were automated within the platform.
  • Full traceability: calculation logic, intermediate results and allocations could be reviewed and explained.
  • Greater consistency: the same rules could be applied repeatedly, reducing reliance on individual interpretation.
  • Institutionalised knowledge: bespoke logic and edge cases were embedded in the platform rather than retained in individual spreadsheets.
  • Scalable operations: the fund gained a stronger foundation for managing growing transaction volumes and structural complexity without proportionately increasing manual effort.

The most important transformation was not simply faster computation.

It was the conversion of a fragile, person-dependent process into a repeatable, controlled and institutionalised operating capability.

Why AI should not be responsible for the final financial number

As AI capabilities advance, there is understandable enthusiasm about using large language models and autonomous agents for fund accounting.

But there is an important distinction between using AI to assist with financial processes and asking AI to independently determine financial results.

Large language models are probabilistic by nature. They are remarkably capable of interpreting documents, understanding context, suggesting approaches and identifying anomalies. But their outputs are not inherently deterministic, and they are not substitutes for controlled financial calculation engines.

For fund managers and administrators, this distinction is critical. An incorrect allocation, an overlooked side-letter provision or an inconsistent carry calculation can create financial, regulatory and reputational consequences.

Our philosophy is simple: use deterministic engines for financial computations, and AI agents for the intelligence, interpretation and workflow automation surrounding them.

This separation allows fund managers to embrace AI without compromising the integrity of core financial calculations.

It also provides a more practical approach to data security and governance. AI capabilities can be introduced selectively, with appropriate data access restrictions, human approvals and auditability, rather than granting an autonomous model unrestricted access to sensitive investor and financial information.

The next frontier: AI agents as virtual fund operations employees

While core calculations remain deterministic, many of the activities surrounding fund accounting still require substantial human effort.

These include interpreting LPAs and side letters, reconciling historical records, identifying inconsistencies, preparing data for upload, investigating exceptions, explaining unexpected movements and coordinating reviews. These are precisely the activities where AI agents have the potential to create substantial value.

CapHive's architecture enables clients to progressively introduce an optional AI and agentic layer around the core application, without changing the deterministic nature of the underlying computations. This operates at two levels: during initial set-up and configuration, and during ongoing fund operations.

Over time, this creates the foundation for something more powerful: virtual fund operations employees.

Rather than simply answering questions through a chatbot, specialised agents can execute increasingly sophisticated, multi-step operational workflows, coordinating with the core platform and escalating exceptions to human teams.

A reconciliation agent, for example, can be tasked with reviewing a quarter-end close, identifying unexplained differences, tracing them to underlying transactions and preparing a structured exception report.

These agents could become available on demand, scaling alongside fund operations rather than being constrained by traditional staffing models.

The goal is not to eliminate the need for experienced fund accountants. It is to allow their expertise to be applied where it matters most: judgment, oversight, exception resolution and investor relationships, instead of repetitive data handling and manual reconciliations.

Institutional memory: an overlooked benefit of automation

Complex funds accumulate institutional knowledge over many years. This includes how unusual transactions are handled, why particular investors receive special treatment, how historical exceptions were resolved and how sophisticated allocation methodologies have evolved.

Much of this knowledge traditionally resides in spreadsheets, email correspondence and the memories of a few senior fund accountants. When individuals leave or responsibilities change, that knowledge can be difficult to recover.

By embedding financial logic, contractual configurations, calculation dependencies and audit trails within the application, CapHive helps preserve this institutional memory.

AI agents can potentially make that knowledge even more accessible by helping teams investigate historical decisions, understand computational logic and navigate exceptions. This is particularly valuable for multi-fund managers, administrators and other organisations managing increasingly complex structures across jurisdictions.

The long-term competitive advantage is not merely automation. It is building an institutional knowledge base that becomes stronger, more accessible and more scalable over time.

An AI-ready operating architecture

Not traditional software or AI. Both, combined well.

The future of fund operations will not be defined by choosing between traditional software and AI. It will be defined by how intelligently the two are combined. Financial computations demand precision, reproducibility and robust controls. Operational workflows increasingly benefit from contextual reasoning, adaptability and autonomous execution.

A deterministic, highly configurable financial computation engine at the core, complemented by an optional AI agentic layer that can progressively automate complex operational workflows.
