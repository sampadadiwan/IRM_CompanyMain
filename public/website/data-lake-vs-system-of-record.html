Data strategy

Data lake or not. CapHive fits either way.

Some funds run a data lake. Others would rather not take on the cost and complexity of one. CapHive feeds a lake cleanly when you have one, and covers every need on its own when you don't.

Nov 5, 2026 · 4 min read · Data strategy

Why funds look at a lake

A large manager's data sits in many places: one or more administrators, a general ledger, a CRM, portfolio company KPI packs, and a good number of spreadsheets. A data lake such as Snowflake or Databricks promises to bring it together, keep it in the firm's own hands, and let analysts work in SQL, notebooks and Power BI.

Those are sensible goals. Two things are worth knowing before choosing how to meet them.

  • Fund data is small. A few thousand LPs, a few hundred portfolio companies and fifteen years of quarters add up to gigabytes, not the billions of rows these platforms are built for. CapHive can export an organisation's entire history as a single SQLite file.
  • A lake is an ongoing commitment. Alongside the platform's usage bill come ingestion and transformation tools, BI licences, and a data engineering team to keep pipelines running as administrators change file layouts and new funds launch with new terms. For many firms the team is the larger cost.

Some firms will want that investment, others won't. CapHive works with both.

Without a lake: CapHive on its own

The data has to be loaded somewhere, so load it once, into a system that checks it on the way in, computes the fund's numbers with one engine, and controls who sees what.

Everything a lake is usually bought for is covered:

No pipelines to maintain and no data team to hire. Your fund rules live in one place as configuration, and stay with the fund when people move on.

With a lake: CapHive feeds it cleanly

If your firm runs a lake, keep it. Put CapHive in front of it as the fund-operations source, and the lake receives fund numbers that are already checked and computed, ready to join with the rest of the firm's data. Investors are still served straight from CapHive.

The lake can take data from CapHive four ways:

  • Scheduled database dumps. A SQLite copy of your organisation's data, at the frequency you set or on demand.
  • The REST API. The same endpoints the application uses, under the same permissions.
  • Report Builder exports. The reports your team already uses.
  • A direct SQL connection. Straight to your own database, on single-tenant installations on AWS, typically for large clients.

Every row carries the id of the record it came from, so the warehouse can trace any number back to its source, and the figures there match what your LPs were sent. Your data team spends its time on analysis, not on rebuilding fee and IRR logic.

Which way suits you

  • A lake makes sense if the firm already runs one, if fund data needs to sit alongside other businesses or public-markets trading data, or if there is a group-wide mandate for a single warehouse.
  • CapHive on its own makes sense if the goal is one reliable view of your funds, investors and portfolio, without the cost of a platform and a team to run it.
  • Either way, the fund's numbers are computed once, in one place, and everything downstream reads the same figures.
