1. What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. When you return, the browser sends the file back, so the site can recognise the session and remember state. Cookies set by the site you are visiting are called first-party cookies. Cookies set by another domain whose content is loaded on the page are called third-party cookies.

Cookies are described by how long they last. A session cookie is deleted when you close the browser. A persistent cookie stays until it expires or you delete it.

This policy also covers similar technologies that do comparable things: local storage and session storage in your browser, pixels and tracking images in web pages or emails, software development kits, and device or browser fingerprinting. Where we say "cookies" below, we mean all of these. One of the entries in our inventory is a local storage item rather than a cookie, and it is marked as such.

2. How we use them on caphive.com

caphive.com is a marketing website, and it uses very few cookies. There are exactly two, both belonging to Google Analytics, and neither is set unless you accept them. We use them:

  • to analyse how the site is used in aggregate — which pages people read, where they arrive from, where they drop off — so we can improve it.

We also record your accept-or-decline choice itself, so we do not ask again on every page. That record is kept in your browser's local storage rather than in a cookie.

We do not use advertising or marketing cookies on this website, we do not use it to build profiles of individual investors, and we do not sell the data cookies produce. If you decline, no analytics cookie is written at all. How we handle the personal data behind these cookies, and the legal bases we rely on, is set out in our Privacy Policy.

3. The categories we use

We group cookies into four categories. Two of the four are not used on this website at all.

3.1 Strictly necessary

Required for the site to function and to be kept secure, including recording your cookie choices. These cannot be switched off through a consent banner. On caphive.com this category contains a single local storage item and no cookies — the public pages are static files, so nothing needs a server-side session.

3.2 Preferences

Remember choices you have made, so the site behaves the way you left it. Not used on caphive.com. The signed-in application does use preference cookies, listed in 3.6 below.

3.3 Analytics

Help us understand how the site is used, in aggregate. We set these only with your consent. Until you accept, our analytics provider runs in a restricted mode in which it stores nothing on your device and reports only anonymous, aggregate page counts that cannot be linked into a session or back to you.

3.4 Marketing

Measure campaign reach and allow advertising partners to show you content elsewhere. We set no marketing cookies, and we run no advertising tags or conversion pixels on this site. These signals are switched off permanently in our analytics configuration rather than left to your choice, so there is nothing here for you to decline.

4. Third-party cookies and what they see

Some content on this site is served by other organisations. When your browser loads that content, the provider can see technical information about your request — your IP address, the page you were on, your browser and device, and the time — and can set its own cookies.

We keep this list deliberately short. There are two, both Google, and no others:

  • Google Analytics (googletagmanager.com) — receives page and event data to produce aggregate usage reports for us. Until you accept, it is loaded in a restricted mode that stores nothing on your device.
  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com) — serves the typefaces the site is set in. It sets no cookies, but it does receive your IP address as part of the request for the font files. This happens on every page whether or not you accept analytics, because the fonts are needed to render the page.

We do not run a content delivery network, an advertising platform, a chat widget, a video embed or a social tracking pixel on this site. There are no iframes on any page.

We do not control Google's cookies and cannot read them. Google uses the data under its own privacy policy and may combine it with data it holds from other sites. Google offers a browser add-on that opts you out of Google Analytics across all websites.

5. Managing your choices

5.1 Your choice on this site

The first time you visit, a banner asks whether you accept analytics cookies. Nothing non-essential is written before you answer, and declining is exactly as easy as accepting — the two buttons sit side by side and neither is pre-selected.

You can change your answer whenever you like: select Cookie settings in the footer of any page and the banner will reopen. Choosing "decline" after having accepted stops any further analytics cookie from being written; to remove cookies already stored, clear them in your browser as described below.

Because there is only one non-essential category on this site, the choice is a single accept-or-decline rather than a per-category panel. If we ever add another category, we will ask again with granular controls.

5.2 Browser controls

Your browser lets you see the cookies stored for a site, delete them, block cookies from particular sites, block all third-party cookies, or clear everything when you close the browser. The settings are usually under Privacy or Security. Vendor instructions are available for Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge in each browser's own help pages.

Two points to keep in mind. Blocking storage for this site may break parts of the signed-in application, which needs its session cookie to keep you authenticated. And because your cookie choice is itself remembered in your browser's storage, clearing everything also clears that choice, and you will be asked again on your next visit.

You can also use your browser's private or incognito mode, which discards cookies when the window closes.

5.3 Withdrawing consent

Where the law requires consent for a cookie, we ask for it before that cookie is set, and you can withdraw it at any time using the Cookie settings link described in 5.1. Strictly necessary items are not covered by that choice. If you cannot find a way to change your choices, write to hello@caphive.com and we will action your request.

6. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

Some browsers can send a "Do Not Track" (DNT) signal. There is no agreed industry standard for how a website should respond to it, so, like most websites, we do not currently change our behaviour in response to a DNT header. We will say so here if that changes.

Global Privacy Control (GPC) is a more recent signal that expresses an opt-out of the sale or sharing of personal data. Our position:

  • We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. There is nothing on this site for a GPC signal to opt you out of.
  • We do not read the GPC header programmatically. In practice the outcome is the same either way: analytics is off until you actively accept it, so a visitor sending GPC who never accepts is never tracked.

Whatever signal your browser sends, you can always control cookies directly using section 5.

7. Changes to this policy

We will update this page when the cookies we use change, when a new third party is added, or when the law changes. The date at the top shows when it was last revised. Where a change means we need consent we do not already have, we will ask again before setting the cookie — the consent banner is versioned, so a material change re-prompts everyone rather than relying on a choice made against an older policy. Material changes take effect 14 days after publication.
