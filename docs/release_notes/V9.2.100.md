# Release Notes — V9.2.100
**Released: 2 October 2026**

---

## What's New

### Mistyped years are stopped before they reach your data
A year typed as `0224` instead of `2024`, or `2204`, used to save without complaint and quietly distort ratio history, allocation periods and investor statements, often weeks before anyone noticed. CapHive now checks key dates against a sensible window: by default, **no more than 20 years before or 20 years after today**. A date outside the window is refused with a message that names the date and the window, for example: *"Valuation date 0224-03-31 must be within 20 years before and 20 years after today."* The window moves with today's date; there is no fixed cut-off year.

The check is **on for every entity** and **blocks** the save or run. It covers:

- **Valuations** — the valuation form, the API and valuation imports
- **Portfolio investments** — the form, the API and portfolio investment imports
- **Generate Fund Ratios** and fund ratio run templates, whether triggered one at a time or by tag
- **Run Allocations**, including the pre-run checks of an allocation group run
- **Investor Statements** generated from a capital commitment, for both the commitment and the investing entity

When a run or statement is refused, no background calculation or document generation starts. In an import, only the row with the bad date fails; the import results show the message against that row.

### Your existing records are left alone
Only new dates, and dates being changed, are checked. If your entity already holds a portfolio investment dated 25 years ago, you can still edit its notes and other fields and save it normally. Changing that date, or entering a new one outside the window, is what gets checked.

**One exception:** the valuation edit form checks the whole valuation, not just what changed. Editing a valuation dated more than 20 years ago through the form is therefore refused, even if you only changed its value. If that happens, widen the window on the valuation rule as described below.

### Set your own window, or switch the check off
Each covered area has its own rule under **Entity → Validation Rules**, named "… Date Out Of Range" (Valuation, Portfolio Investment, Fund Ratio, Fund Ratio Run Template, Allocation Run and Investor Statement). A company admin can change, for your entity only:

- **past_years** and **future_years** — how far back and ahead dates may go (both default to 20). A blank or non-numeric value is treated as 20.
- **Severity** — turn the block into a warning you can confirm. Warnings appear on the valuation form; run screens do not show warnings, so a warning there lets the run go ahead.
- **Enabled** — switch the check off.

Other entities are not affected by your settings.

**Back-dating portfolio investments:** adding a buy also records a valuation on the investment date, so the valuation rule applies too. To enter buys older than 20 years (for example, for a 2003-vintage fund), widen or switch off **both** the Portfolio Investment and the Valuation rules.

---

## What to Do

| If you use... | Action |
|---|---|
| Valuations, portfolio investments, fund ratios, allocations or Investor Statements | No action needed for dates within 20 years of today. If a date is refused, check it for a mistyped year. |
| Funds holding genuinely older data | A company admin raises **past_years** (for example, to 30) on the relevant rules under **Entity → Validation Rules**, or switches the rule off. For portfolio investment buys, change both the Portfolio Investment and the Valuation rules. |
| Deploying this release (engineering) | Run `bin/rails rules:seed_defaults` after the code deploy, separately on **IN** and on **US**. The new checks do nothing until the rules are seeded. To roll back without a deploy, switch the six "… Date Out Of Range" rules off. |
