1. What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. When you return, the browser sends the file back, so the site can recognise the session and remember state. Cookies set by the site you are visiting are called first-party cookies. Cookies set by another domain whose content is loaded on the page are called third-party cookies.

Cookies are described by how long they last. A session cookie is deleted when you close the browser. A persistent cookie stays until it expires or you delete it.

This policy also covers similar technologies that do comparable things: local storage and session storage in your browser, pixels and tracking images in web pages or emails, software development kits, and device or browser fingerprinting. Where we say "cookies" below, we mean all of these.

2. How we use them on caphive.com

caphive.com is a marketing website. We use cookies for a small number of narrow purposes:

  • to make the site work — for example to keep a form submission secure and to route your request to the right server;
  • to remember a preference you have set, such as your cookie choices, so we do not ask again on every page;
  • to analyse how the site is used in aggregate — which pages people read, where they arrive from, where they drop off — so we can improve it; and
  • to measure whether our campaigns and event invitations reach the right audience, where you have allowed it.

We do not use cookies on this website to build profiles of individual investors, and we do not sell the data cookies produce. How we handle the personal data behind these cookies, and the legal bases we rely on, is set out in our Privacy Policy.

3. The categories we use

We group cookies into four categories.

3.1 Strictly necessary

Required for the site to function and to be kept secure — load balancing, session integrity, form protection against cross-site request forgery, and recording your cookie choices. These are set on our own legitimate interest in operating the site, and cannot be switched off through a consent banner. If you block them in your browser, parts of the site may stop working.

3.2 Preferences

Remember choices you have made, so the site behaves the way you left it.

3.3 Analytics

Help us understand how the site is used, in aggregate. We set these only with your consent where consent is required.

3.4 Marketing

Measure campaign reach and, where applicable, allow us or our advertising partners to show you relevant content elsewhere. We set these only with your consent.

5. Managing your choices

5.1 Browser controls

Your browser lets you see the cookies stored for a site, delete them, block cookies from particular sites, block all third-party cookies, or clear everything when you close the browser. The settings are usually under Privacy or Security. Vendor instructions are available for Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge in each browser's own help pages.

Two points to keep in mind. Blocking strictly necessary cookies may break parts of this site. And because an opt-out is itself remembered in a cookie, clearing all cookies also clears your opt-out, and you will be asked again.

You can also use your browser's private or incognito mode, which discards cookies when the window closes, and — on many platforms — reset or limit the advertising identifier in your device settings.

5.2 Consent

Where the law requires consent for a cookie, we ask for it before that cookie is set, and you can withdraw it at any time. Strictly necessary cookies are not covered by that choice. If you cannot find a way to change your choices on this site, write to hello@caphive.com and we will action your request.

6. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

Some browsers can send a "Do Not Track" (DNT) signal. There is no agreed industry standard for how a website should respond to it, so, like most websites, we do not currently change our behaviour in response to a DNT header. We will say so here if that changes.

Global Privacy Control (GPC) is a more recent signal that expresses an opt-out of the sale or sharing of personal data. Our position is set out below.
