The problem

A transaction is only as good as the thread it lives in.

Deals and secondaries are the most document-heavy, most multi-party events a fund runs — and they are usually run in the least structured tools available.

Pipeline and documents live apart

The stage sits in one tool and the diligence file sits in another. Nobody can open a deal and see both the position and the paperwork behind it.

A secondary with 40 sellers becomes 40 email threads

Pricing, consents and transfer forms are chased individually. One seller going quiet stalls a settlement nobody can see the state of.

Approvals are impossible to evidence afterwards

Six months on, the IC approval is in a slide deck, the sign-off is in an inbox, and the signature is in a PDF nobody can date with confidence.

Capabilities

Pipeline, vehicle and transfer.

Sourcing, execution and settlement sit on the same record. A diligence request, an IC approval and a transfer of units are all the same deal seen at different stages.

Deal Pipeline & Diligence

From first meeting to IC approval.

Every deal carries its own stage, room and checklist. The work is visible, the documents are versioned, and the decision is recorded where the deal is.

  • Configurable stages with owners, next steps and stale-deal flags
  • Deal rooms with versioned documents and per-party access
  • Document requests tracked by counterparty, not by inbox
  • Diligence checklists with findings attached to the item they answer
  • IC memos, approvals and conditions recorded against the deal
  • Full audit trail — who saw what, who approved what, and when

Co-investments & SPVs

Spin up a vehicle in the same system.

Secondaries — Funds & Direct

Many sellers, one settlement.

LP stake transfers and direct secondaries run as a single coordinated transaction — matching, pricing, consent and paperwork tracked per party until the register updates.

  • LP stake transfers in funds and direct secondaries in companies
  • Multi-party matching of buyers and sellers within one transaction
  • Pricing, discounts and allocation across a fragmented seller base
  • GP consent, ROFR and transfer paperwork tracked per counterparty
  • Settlement, and the register or capital accounts updated on close
