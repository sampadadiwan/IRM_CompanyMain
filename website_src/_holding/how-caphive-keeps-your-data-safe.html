How CapHive keeps your fund's data safe.

The questions your investors and diligence teams ask about your data on CapHive, answered in plain terms, with the safeguards you can see and switch on yourself.

Nov 10, 2026 · 6 min read · Security

Your fund runs on CapHive: capital accounts, investor bank details, KYC documents, valuations you haven't published yet. Your investors and your diligence teams will ask how that is protected. These are the questions we hear most, answered in plain terms, with what you can see and switch on yourself.

The short version

No single safeguard stands alone. Every request has to get past several checks, each working on its own, so a problem in one is still stopped by the next.

Who can see our fund's data?

  • Only your firm. Many firms use CapHive, and each one sees only its own data, on every screen, report, export, dashboard, the API and AI connections.
  • Within your firm, only the people you choose. Your admins decide who sees which funds, investors and documents, and who can approve.
  • Your investors see only their own accounts: their commitments, notices and documents, nothing about anyone else.
  • We test it the way someone would try to break it. Our automated tests sign in as one firm and try to open another firm's records. They must be refused, every time.

Can CapHive's own staff see our data?

  • Only when you let us. Our support team can reach your firm's data only while support access is switched on by your team (the Enable support switch), and you can switch it off at any time.
  • You'll see when we do. When support signs in to help, it shows in your admins' notification bell and on your Security Events screen. Your admins are also emailed whenever support access is switched on or off.
  • Every member of our team who can access client accounts uses two-factor sign-in, and each person has only the access their job needs.

Could someone redirect an investor's money?

Changing where an investor is paid needs two people, and the investor is told.

For example, Meera, on your team, updates an investor's bank account from …4821 to …7310:

  1. The account's Bank Verified tick is cleared at once.
  2. The investor gets an email that their bank details changed, and what to do if they didn't ask for it.
  3. Your other KYC approvers are asked to review it.
  4. Meera can't verify her own change. Ravi, another approver, checks the new account and verifies it.
  5. Until then, any distribution to that investor is flagged, so your team sees it before money goes out.

This covers the account number and IFSC code, and SWIFT or IBAN fields you mark as payout details. A firm with a single approver can still confirm a change, after a clear warning, and it is recorded as such. We recommend naming a second approver.

Can one person push a capital call or distribution through alone?

Not if you switch on maker–checker, which we recommend for any firm with two or more approvers:

  • Whoever prepared a call or distribution can't also approve it; they ask another approver with one click.
  • If the figures change after approval, the approval is withdrawn and has to be given again.
  • Every approved call or distribution shows who prepared it and who approved it.

How would we know if something unusual happened?

Your company admins have a Security Events screen, under Settings. It shows your firm's sign-ins, failed sign-ins, role changes, two-factor changes, API keys, support access and more.

  • Every entry explains itself: when it's normal, and what to do if it isn't.
  • The important changes come to you. Your admins are emailed when someone is given or loses an admin or approver role, for example "Approver role given to Rahul Mehta".
  • Kept for over a year, so you can look back across a full audit cycle.
  • We watch too. Our systems alert our team to unusual activity, and we review a weekly summary of security events.

How is our investors' personal data protected?

  • Kept out of logs and error reports. Tax IDs, bank details and sign-in details are removed before anything is written to a log or an error report.
  • Keys stored encrypted. Keys your firm saves for connected services are encrypted, and only their last few characters are ever shown.
  • Your data stays in your region. India and US clients run on separate regional environments.

How do you keep it secure over time?

  • Regular security reviews against the OWASP Top 10, the industry's standard list of the ways web applications are attacked.
  • The most serious findings are fixed first, and every fix is re-tested before it ships.
  • Fixes stay fixed. Each one gets automated tests that try the problem again and confirm your normal work is unaffected; they run on every future change.
  • Security is part of every new feature, checked against the same list before it's built, not added afterwards.

What you can do today

  1. Turn on two-factor sign-in for every user, starting with your admins and approvers.
  2. Name at least two approvers, and switch on maker–checker for capital calls and distributions. Ask us and we'll set it up.
  3. Keep admin and approver roles tight. Give them to the people who need them, and remove them when someone changes role or leaves.
  4. Switch support access on only when you need our help, and off again afterwards.
  5. Glance at Security Events now and then. A sign-in or role change you don't recognise is worth a question.
  6. Tell us about anything odd at support@caphive.com. We'd much rather hear about something harmless than miss something real.

No one can honestly promise a system is beyond attack. What we promise is that we keep checking, keep fixing, and keep you able to see what is happening in your own account.
