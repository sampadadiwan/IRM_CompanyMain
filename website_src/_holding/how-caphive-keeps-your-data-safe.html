How CapHive keeps your fund's data safe.

You trust us with capital accounts, investor bank details, identity documents and valuations that have not been published yet. Here is how we protect them, in plain terms, and the controls you can use yourself.

Nov 10, 2026 · 6 min read · Security

Several layers, not one wall

No single control should be all that stands between someone and your data. So we build in layers. Each one works on its own, and if one of them ever had a problem, the others would still be in the way.

We review ourselves regularly, and keep the fixes fixed

  • Regular security reviews. We review the platform against the OWASP Top 10, the industry's standard list of the most common ways web applications are attacked, looking at it the way an attacker would.
  • Fixed by severity. Findings are ranked, and the most serious are fixed first. Every fix is re-tested before it ships.
  • Two automated tests per fix. One tries the problem again and must be refused. The other proves your normal work still runs exactly as before.
  • They stay in our test suite. A later change that brought a problem back would fail them.

Your firm's data is only ever your firm's

Many firms use CapHive, but each one sees only its own data. This is the rule we guard most closely.

  • Every record belongs to one firm. Screens, reports, exports, dashboards, the API and AI connections all check that before showing anything.
  • LPs see only their own accounts. An investor signing in sees their own commitments, notices and documents, nothing else.
  • Checked automatically. Our tests sign in as one firm and try to open another firm's records. They must be refused, every time.

The most important thing a shared platform does is keep each firm's data apart. So we test it the way someone would try to break it.

Changes that move money need a second person

When someone changes where an investor's money goes, another approver in your firm confirms it, and the investor hears about it.

Example. Meera updates an investor's bank account from …4821 to …7310.

  1. The Bank Verified tick is cleared straight away.
  2. The investor gets an email saying their bank details changed, and what to do if they didn't ask for it.
  3. Your other KYC approvers get a "please review" email.
  4. Meera's own Verify button is greyed out, with a tooltip saying why.
  5. Ravi, another approver, checks the new account and verifies it.

This covers the account number, the IFSC code, and any SWIFT or IBAN field marked as a payout field on your forms. Distributions flag any bank change that hasn't been confirmed yet, so your team sees it before money goes out. If your firm has only one approver, they can confirm their own change after an extra warning, and that is recorded.

Maker–checker on capital calls and distributions is there when your firm wants it:

  • Whoever prepared a call or distribution can't also approve it.
  • They press Request approval to email your other approvers.
  • If the figures change after approval, the approval is withdrawn.
  • Every approved call or distribution shows who prepared it.

Our own access to your account

  • Two-factor for our staff. Everyone on our team who can sign in to client accounts must use two-factor authentication.
  • Only the access each person needs. Our staff are given the narrowest access their job requires, and no more.
  • You decide. Our support team can sign in to help a user only while that user's Enable support switch is on. You can turn it off at any time.
  • You can see it. When support signs in to help, it appears in your admins' notification bell and on the Security Events screen. Your admins are emailed whenever the Enable support switch changes.

See it for yourself: Security Events

Company admins have a Security Events screen, under Settings. It lists sign-ins, failed sign-ins, role changes, two-factor changes, API tokens, support access and more, for your firm only.

  • Every row explains itself. A "What to do" button says when the event is normal and what to do if it isn't.
  • Alerts for the changes that matter. Your admins are emailed when someone is given or loses an admin or approver role, for example "Approver role given to Rahul Mehta".
  • Kept for over a year, so you can look back across a full audit cycle.
  • Reviewed by us too. Our own team reviews a weekly summary of security events.

Personal data, handled with care

  • Kept out of logs and error reports. Tax IDs, bank details and sign-in information are filtered out before anything is written to a log or an error report.
  • Secrets encrypted. Keys your firm stores for connected services are kept encrypted.
  • No real client data in development. When data is copied for building and testing the product, real personal details such as tax IDs and bank accounts are removed from the copy.
  • Your region, your data. India and US clients run on separate regional environments, and data stays in its own region.
  • Monitored. Our systems watch for unusual activity and alert our team when they see it.

What you can do

  1. Turn on two-factor authentication for every user, starting with your admins and approvers. Switching it off later needs the password and a current code.
  2. Look at Security Events now and then. A role change or sign-in you don't recognise is worth a question.
  3. Use maker–checker on capital calls and distributions if you have two or more approvers. Ask us and we will switch it on for your firm.
  4. Keep admin and approver roles tight. Give them to the people who need them, and remove them when someone changes role or leaves.
  5. Switch Enable support on only when you need help, and off again afterwards.
  6. Tell us about anything odd at support@caphive.com. We would much rather hear about something harmless than miss something real.

Security is never finished, and we don't claim any system is beyond attack. What we can promise is that we keep checking, keep fixing, and keep you able to see what is happening in your own account.
